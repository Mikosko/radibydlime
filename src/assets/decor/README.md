# Editorial ornament assets

These transparent PNG crops come from the Rádi bydlíme botanical and icon concept sheets supplied for the September 2026 article-page exploration. They are authored presentation assets for the site, not media-catalog photographs.

`social-*.png` contains paired default and `-hover` production crops from the supplied social-icon sheets. The olive states are visible at rest; their platform-color partners appear on pointer hover and keyboard focus. `footer.png` is the supplied text-free landscape source. `footer-landscape.png` is its cleaned production crop: it preserves the open upper canvas for HTML content and removes transparent padding below the ground line. Visible wording remains HTML in the layout.

`brand-botanical.png` is the optimized production derivative of the compact branching flower supplied in the inspiration folder. [BrandMark](../../components/brand-mark/SPEC.md) positions it above the final letters of both wordmark variants.

`footer-border-botanical.png` is the transparent production derivative of the supplied meadow-line crop. The site layout mirrors it so the footer's upper rule leads into the flowers at its right edge.

`torn-paper.png` is a transparent blank handmade-paper texture generated in September 2026 from the supplied pre-footer reference as visual guidance. The generation requested a warm ivory horizontal sheet with soft fibrous deckled edges and subtle pulp texture, and explicitly excluded text, illustrations, botanical marks, borders, and an opaque background. The site layers editable HTML text and a separate botanical asset over it.

`botanical-yarrow.png`, `botanical-daisies.png`, `botanical-bellflower.png`, and `botanical-coneflower.png` are transparent decorative cutouts generated with the built-in image tool from the supplied botanical collection sheet. Each prompt requested a high-fidelity isolation of one named illustration, preservation of its dark olive hand-drawn linework and proportions, comfortable transparent padding, and removal of all surrounding paper, wording, rules, logos, hearts, and other plants. They form a small varied ornament set; they are not a botanical identification reference.

Use them as quiet, non-semantic support for editorial hierarchy. Render them with empty alternative text, keep them subordinate to content, and avoid making any ornament necessary to understand or navigate the page.
